# Figure guide (v2 model, 26-county)

All figures are in `figures/v2/` and are regenerated by `python src/report.py --scope 26`
after `sweep.py --version v2` and `validate.py --version v2`.

Colour roles are fixed across figures: **blue** = anchored/optimised groups or a node
that helps; **grey** = today's WDT groups or context; **orange** = conventional plant;
**red** = a problem (limit, wrong sign, new failure). "Validation" always means 5 unseen
weather seeds × thermal ratings 100/95/90%, with identical frozen cases for every grouping.

---

### fig1_line458_diagnosis.png: why Poolbeg South – Carrickmines overloads
**Left.** The average flow on the line in the N-1 state that overloads it (Inchicore –
Irishtown out), split by source. Demand pulls about 650 MW towards Dublin, and
renewables and plant push back, so the net flow sits past the −254 MW limit.
**Right.** Every node sorted by how much 1 MW of reduction relieves the line. Only about
25 of 188 nodes help. The strongest by far is turning **Irishtown down** (orange square).

*Takeaway:* the overload is caused by demand, so curtailing wind alone cannot fix it.
Relief needs conventional plant in the group, and the replacement MW spread across
the fleet (the distributed slack).

### fig2_anchor_selection.png: which lines become anchors
The 16 most problematic monitored lines, measured two ways. **Left:** share of
"binding" events (the worst line at each step of the relief loop). **Right:** share of
snapshots where the line is overloaded *before* any relief. The right-hand measure is
needed because the relief loop stops at the worst line, which hides the others. A line
becomes an anchor if it is at or above 1% on either measure and some grouping can
relieve it. Light grey = no grouping can relieve it (Corkagh – Castlebagot).

### fig3_anchor_map.png: where the anchors are
The 11 anchor lines, numbered as in every other figure. Most sit in the
Dublin / Meath / Kildare corridor, which is where the right-hand panel zooms in.

### fig4_groups_initial.png / fig4_groups_optimised.png: the constraint groups themselves
One small map per anchor (black line). Coloured markers are the group's members:
blue farms help that line, red farms have the wrong sign, orange squares are
conventional units. Marker area scales with capacity. Each panel's footer gives the
member count, total MW and the capacity-weighted mean σ (MW of relief per MW reduced).
"Initial" is the shift-factor starting point. "Optimised" is the annealed headline
configuration.

### fig5_validation.png: does it work on unseen weather?
One row per validation case. **Left:** renewable dispatch-down, WDT today (grey) vs the
anchored groups (blue), with the change in percentage points. **Middle:** the
conventional redispatch the anchored groups use for relief. WDT uses none, because
its groups contain only wind and solar. **Right:** the share of snapshots that are
secure after relief.

*Takeaway:* dispatch-down falls in every case, and security rises from about 49% to
about 82%, paid for with conventional redispatch.

### fig6_lambda_tradeoff.png: what the coupling constants do
Each dot is one λ configuration (validation means): renewable dispatch-down against
conventional redispatch. With the conventional term switched off ("renewable DD
only"), the optimiser pushes relief onto plant, reaching 4.5% DD at 10.4%
redispatch. Pricing redispatch (λ_C) pulls the solutions back into the 5.7–6.4% band.
The star is today's WDT groups: less redispatch, but only because half the
overloads are left unrelieved.

### fig7_group_fairness.png: no more one-farm groups
Group sizes in nodes (left) and MW (right). v1, with no floor and λ_N = 0.5, produced
single-farm groups. v2, with a hard floor of 5 nodes and λ_N = 0.1, spreads every
constraint across at least 5 nodes and 130+ MW.

### fig8_annealing.png: the optimisation run
The headline run over ~6k ensemble evaluations. From the top: energy E (current and
best), then renewable DD and conventional redispatch of the current grouping, then the
temperature schedule (log scale), then the share of moves accepted and the share
rejected by the security guard.

### fig9_sigma_by_group.png: how well members help their own line
Per anchor, each member's σ (symlog axis; positive = helps). Grey = initial group,
blue = optimised; the black bar is the capacity-weighted mean. Optimisation mostly keeps
or raises the mean. A few wrong-sign members survive, because the wrong-sign penalty is
soft.
